Taekwondo and Belonging at Nazarbayev University

Research question

How do NU students who practice taekwondo experience a sense of belonging to NU student life in the 2026-2027 academic year?

예의

예의Courtesy

Epistemological positioning

Positioning: interpretivist. My question asks how NU students who practise taekwondo experience a sense of belonging, which means I am after personal meaning rather than measurable facts — exactly what interpretivism is for. Belonging is a subjective, socially constructed idea, so I can only understand it through students' own accounts of their lives at NU and in the club. And because the answer depends on this specific context — this university, this club, these students — my question calls for depth from a few participants rather than generalisation from many.

Literature review

Astin states that students that involve in activities have better chances of staying integrated in campus life, Moxey found that on the first place students actually are more engaged to their clubs and via it they deepen their ties with campus community. However, Astin found out that heavy athletic exposure of students may distance them from campus life. Real clash is that Astin states that their high exposure to trainings may distance them while Moxey states that club is a way to connect with campus life.

Kim et al. (2021) made meta analysis of 28 studies and most of them showed positive results regarding sociality and school life adjustments, including making new friends. Kim et al. (2014) has interviewed exactly 12 active taekwondo practitioners and found out that their friendships across different cultures have strengthened. The first measures with questionnaires; the second asks members about their experience.

However, Kim et al. (2021) was examining almost all Korean schoolchildren with concepts the authors admit were classified inconsistently across studies. Kim et al. (2014) studied commercial studios with Korean masters. Moxey studied only one UK university during COVID. There were no authors who made such research in Kazakhstan, and in a student-led taekwondo club.

Analytical framework

Sensitising concepts

By belonging I state that your presence matters, you are a member, and you share something with others, it may be pain during trainings or achievements during tournaments, or getting a new belt with your friends. Similar belts as well as dobok is what binds. My concept is shared physical hardship during taekwondo trainings.

Expectations

Old members describe belonging as shared tournaments, shared harsh trainings, while new members describe belonging as eating together after training, celebrating each other's birthdays, and being happy for each other's achievements in taekwondo.

Positionality

I am a new member of the club with previous 9 years of experience. This gives me some background about taekwondo. Access to new people who are also related to taekwondo. But it also connects taekwondo with campus life at NU. I already know what taekwondo may give you. Some members may be concerned that I already have an experience in it. So I will ask people who have just started taekwondo at university club or who have also just joined the club.

AI transparency statement

AI helped me to narrow down my Research Question, and assisted in changing my positivist question into interpretivist. Helped me evaluate my sources and highlighted some tensions between authors that even I did not see. It gave me valid feedback throughout my presentation building.

I chose the topic myself. I found all 6 sources on my own via NU library and checked for their pdf version availability. Every section of the research presentation was written by me. My curiosity came out of a real taekwondo club at NU. While I was searching for sources in our NU library, I wanted to make an experiment and ask AI to find me relevant sources to find out whether AI provided sources will actually suit or even be real time works, but it resulted in false data.
